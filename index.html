Hi, I'm Adithya

I'm a physical AI engineer helping robots learn to see, think, understand, and touch the world.

I also get them running on the robot itself: small chips, tight power budgets, real-time deadlines.

Currently in 📍 München, building robot foundation models at Neura Robotics. It's a long story ↓

Robots used to be programmed. Now they're starting to be taught.

Show a robot enough demonstrations, give it a camera and a sentence, and it can learn to act. I'm curious about what comes after that — when robots don't just see and listen, but also feel, anticipate, and work safely next to people.

How I got here

2012 – 2019engineering, then data

Cars, then data.

I studied automotive engineering and found that the part I loved most was the software deciding what the machine should do. At Accenture and EMIS Health I worked on telecom data, speech models for NHS emergency calls, and the data lake that fed them.

a model is only as good as the pipeline behind it
2020 – 2022a master's in robotics

Robots, finally.

A master's in Advanced Robotics at École Centrale de Nantes, where I built my robotics foundations: kinematics, dynamics, control, sensors, AI and humanoids. My favourite project used motion capture and force plates to have a humanoid copy a person's upper body in real time.

first time a robot moved because I moved
2022bio-signals at Reliev

A detour through the brain and heart.

At Reliev I worked with ECG, EEG and other bio-signals to detect epileptic seizures, with deep learning on top of careful signal processing. I also built the pipeline that handled ECG data at scale, from ingestion and cleaning to training, all aimed at making life safer for people living with epilepsy.

same question as robotics: what is this signal trying to tell me?
2022 – 2024autonomous mobility

Machines on the move.

At Huber Automotive I built camera, LiDAR and radar perception for autonomous vehicles on tight embedded budgets, and worked with Fraunhofer IPA on VeoPipe, a pipeline for AI you can actually certify.

2024 – nowhumanoids

Teaching humanoids.

At Neura Robotics I fine-tune Vision-Language-Action models on teleoperation data, give them a sense of touch, and get them running on Jetson and Qualcomm hardware fast enough to close a real control loop.

Working with me

I'm happiest on problems where a model works in a demo but not yet in the field. I usually deliver two things: the working system, and the measurements that explain what was going wrong before. When I'm exploring a new team, these are the questions I like to ask.

  1. Ship first, or polish first?

    ↓

    I lean towards shipping early and learning from real use, but I care about craft too. I'd like to know where the balance sits.

  2. What happens when something fails in the field?

    ↓

    Whether failures get traced back to a root cause and shared openly, or quietly patched and forgotten. How a team handles failure says a lot about it.

  3. What would I own?

    ↓

    I do my best work when I'm responsible for an outcome, not just a list of tasks.

  4. How do people grow here?

    ↓

    Whether senior engineers make time for others, and whether there's room for me to mentor someone who could one day take over my work.

  5. Is whole-system thinking valued, and what shapes how engineers work together?

    ↓

    Some of the best fixes come from stepping back and looking at the whole pipeline. I'm curious which habits, reviews and rituals are deliberate here.

I'm always happy to chat, whether that's about robots, research, or interesting problems.
